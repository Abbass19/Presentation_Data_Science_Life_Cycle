### The Paper 

The 2025 JMIR study by Koh et al. evaluated whether large language models (LLMs) could automatically classify patient complaints according to the Healthcare Complaints Analysis Tool for General Practice (HCAT-GP) without additional training. Using human-coded complaints as ground truth, the authors prompted **GPT-3.5 Turbo**, **GPT-4o mini**, **GPT-4o**, and **Claude 3.5 Sonnet** in **zero-shot** mode to label each text by domain, category, severity, stage of care, and patient harm. Performance was assessed by comparing AI-generated labels to human codes using **percent concordance** and **Cohen’s κ**. Results showed that GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet achieved the highest overall agreement—**average concordance ≈ 68.8%**, compared with **61.9%** for GPT-3.5. By field, GPT-4o reached **79.4% (κ = 0.623)** for domain, **69.8% (κ = 0.571)** for category, **53.9% (κ = 0.226)** for severity, **66.1% (κ = 0.534)** for stage of care, and **74.2% (κ = 0.162)** for patient harm—generally outperforming GPT-3.5 and comparable to Claude 3.5. Statistical tests (McNemar p < .001 in several fields) confirmed significant differences between models. The study thus demonstrated that contemporary LLMs can approximate human complaint classification with moderate to substantial agreement, positioning them as feasible automated coders for healthcare quality monitoring.

#### Subcategory (27):  We Need to Merge Labels if this has to Work

- **Type:** fine-grained single-label or multi-class; often sparse.
- **Complexity:** high — subjective and low inter-rater reliability historically.
- **Signal:** specific terminology; often short or ambiguous.
- **Distribution:** heavy long-tail; many rare classes.
- **Metrics focus:** per-class F1 for common classes; macro F1 will be depressed by rare classes.
- **Actions if low performance:**
    - **Aggregate**: consider folding rare subcategories into “other” or into parent categories to improve reliability.
    - Active re-labeling: sample frequent confusions and clarify label guidelines.
    - Multi-stage prediction: predict category first, then subcategory only if confidence high.
        
    - Use label smoothing and class-weighting; consider few-shot fine-tuning of BERT on subcategory examples.
        
- **Consequence if used-as-is:** noisy insights, false operational signals—avoid automated high-impact actions based solely on subcategory.
